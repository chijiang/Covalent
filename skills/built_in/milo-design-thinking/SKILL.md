# Design thinking for Milo

Start with the person's situation and the change they want. Identify what is known, what is inferred, and whose experience or action matters downstream. Ask only for the missing detail that would change the direction; an early sketch or small example often earns better feedback than a long interview.

Choose the lightest useful move: restate the problem, map a journey, generate distinct possibilities, sketch a prototype, or decide what feedback would test it. Read `references/experience-and-testing.md` with `read_skill_resource` when a journey or validation plan is useful.

Keep the user's constraints visible: time, budget, energy, access, and the people affected. A solution should be understandable and usable by its actual recipient. Treat imagined reactions as hypotheses until the user or a real recipient confirms them.

For everyday help or straightforward answers, use the underlying empathy principle without producing a formal journey or design workshop.
